Mathematical Formulation of Linear Regression

How Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) models the relationship between study hours and exam scores

Marks (y) = m × Study_Hours (X) + c
Slope (m): {{ metrics.get('slope', 7.75) }}

The marginal increase in marks expected for each additional hour of daily study. Here, every extra hour of revision yields approximately ~7.75 marks.

Intercept (c): {{ metrics.get('intercept', 16.19) }}

The expected baseline exam score for zero reported daily study hours, representing foundational prior knowledge or guessing baseline.

Loss Function: MSE

Ordinary Least Squares minimizes the sum of squared differences between actual student marks and the model's line of best fit.

Dataset Assumptions & Model Limitations

Honest reflection on real-world factors beyond simple linear models

  • Single-Feature Simplicity: The current model relies solely on daily study hours. In real academic environments, student scores are influenced by syllabus difficulty, sleep hygiene, quality of instruction, exam anxiety, and health.
  • Diminishing Marginal Returns: Real learning often exhibits a plateau effect where studying beyond 10-12 hours in a single day leads to cognitive exhaustion, whereas pure linear models continue to predict higher marks linearly.
  • Boundary Clamping: The system implements sensible boundary constraints ($0 \le \text{Marks} \le 100$) so inputs cannot predict negative scores or impossible marks over 100.
  • Educational Guidance Only: Predictions are indicative statistical estimates designed for portfolio demonstration, motivation, and planning.
